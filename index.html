Clip Pipeline

A private, single-user tool that turns Twitch clips into captioned short-form video.

What it does

Clip Pipeline runs on its operator's own hardware. It watches a short list of Twitch channels whose published terms permit clip creation, downloads clips that pass a view and velocity threshold, and checks each one with a local vision model — rejecting screen captures and clips that already carry someone else's burned-in subtitles.

Accepted clips are transcribed locally, cropped to a vertical format, given burned-in captions and a short written commentary, and uploaded to a single YouTube channel that the operator owns. Every upload credits the source stream's title and URL in the description, and every upload starts as private.

What it is not

It is not a public service, it has no users other than its operator, and it does not read, collect or publish anything relating to third parties' accounts. It talks to exactly two APIs: Twitch (to list and fetch clips) and YouTube (to upload to the operator's own channel).

Why it needs Google authorisation

The only Google scope requested is youtube.upload — the narrowest scope that can upload a video. The application cannot read the channel's analytics, its comments, its private data, or any other Google account. The refresh token is stored on the operator's own machine, in a local configuration file that never leaves it.
